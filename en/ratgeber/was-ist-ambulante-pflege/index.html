Care guide · The basics

What is domiciliary care?

Domiciliary care means that people receive support in their own home, with care workers visiting at agreed times rather than requiring a move into residential care.

How does domiciliary care work?

A person remains in their own home, while care workers visit at agreed times and help with activities they cannot manage independently. The frequency and scope of visits depend on the care needs and the support the provider can arrange.

What types of assistance are available?

Personal care (Grundpflege) can include washing, dressing and basic daily routines. Prescribed nursing (Behandlungspflege) concerns medical nursing procedures authorised by a doctor. Household and other practical support are separate areas.

Compare personal care and prescribed nursing

What is the difference from a care home?

With domiciliary care, a person continues living in their own familiar surroundings. Residential care provides accommodation and support within a care facility. The appropriate option depends on health, the amount of help required and individual preferences.

What should families check before choosing a provider?

  • Whether the provider can deliver the particular type of care needed
  • Whether the patient's address lies within its service area
  • Which languages staff speak
  • Which costs may be covered by insurance or payable privately
  • What visiting times and capacity are available
Must domiciliary care take place every day?

Not necessarily. The frequency depends on individual needs, the agreed arrangement and availability.

Can a care service take over everything a family carer does?

That depends on the situation. The tasks to be performed and division of responsibilities need to be agreed with the person, relatives and relevant healthcare professionals.
